Screening Research Prototype

Deep learning triage for early diabetic retinopathy prioritization.

An automated screening system using a fine-tuned ResNet-18 with weighted cross-entropy to estimate DR severity (Stages 0–3: No DR, Mild, Moderate, Severe) and prioritize high-risk, sight-threatening cases for prompt specialist ophthalmic evaluation.

Methodology

End-to-end research pipeline

How images are preprocessed, augmented, and classified through our deep transfer-learning workflow.

1

Preprocessing

Retinal fundus scans are resized to 224×224 pixels and standardized using ImageNet RGB mean and variance vectors with augmentation.

2

Backbone Fine-Tuning

ResNet-18 pretrained on ImageNet. The final 1000-class layer is replaced with a custom projection layer trained with Adam (lr=1e-4).

3

Imbalance Handling

Weighted cross-entropy and a weighted random sampler mitigate class skew, boosting sensitivity for high-risk pathology.

4

Combined Severe Triage

Evaluated on 550 test images with severe and proliferative DR grouped to prioritize high-risk cases for specialist review.

Scientific Responsibility

Limitations & clinical safety scope

This system is an academic research demonstration designed to explore machine-assisted triage. It is not an autonomous medical device.

Screening Scope Only

The model is trained strictly to highlight potential anomalies for prioritization. It cannot diagnose or rule out non-DR ocular conditions such as glaucoma or age-related macular degeneration.

Cohort & Demographic Bias

Trained on the APTOS 2019 dataset representing a specific geographical cohort. Performance may shift across varied camera optics, pupil dilation levels, or varying retinal pigmentations.

Image Quality Sensitivity

Artifacts such as cataract opacities, lens dust, or improper illumination can distort spatial activations. Poor-quality fundus photos must always trigger human re-capture.
